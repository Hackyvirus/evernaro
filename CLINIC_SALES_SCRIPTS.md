# Clinic/Dental Sales Scripts

Companion to the 90-day plan. Two scripts: the first-visit demo, and the day 12-13 trial-to-paid call. Both are meant to be said in your own words, not read verbatim — the structure matters more than the wording.

---

## 1. The 5-Minute Walk-In Demo

**Goal of this visit:** not a sale. Get a 14-day free trial started, or a firm "come back at a specific time." A clinic owner mid-consultation won't give you more than 5 minutes — respect that and you get invited back.

### Opening (15 seconds)
"Hi, I'm Sushant — I build software for clinics in Pune. Do you have 5 minutes? I want to show you one thing on my phone, not sell you anything today."

If they say no: "No problem — when's a quieter time, morning or evening?" Get a specific day/time before you leave. A vague "call me" is a no.

### The pain question (don't skip this — it's what makes the demo land)
"Quick question first — on a normal day, how many patients don't show up for their appointment?"

Let them answer. Whatever number they give, reflect it back in money: "So if a consultation is ₹[X] and you lose [N] slots a week, that's roughly ₹[X×N×4]/month just from no-shows — does that sound about right?"

This does two things: it's *their* number, not your pitch, and it primes the wow moment.

### The demo itself (2 minutes max)
Show these two things, in this order, on your phone:

1. **The patient's view first.** Open a demo queue link (`/business/demo-clinic/queue`) or booking page (`/business/demo-clinic/book`) on your phone and hand it to them. Let them tap through it themselves — "This is what your patient sees. No app to download, they just get a link on WhatsApp."
2. **The reminder.** Show a sample WhatsApp reminder message: "This goes out automatically 24 hours before their appointment. If they don't confirm or don't show up, we automatically follow up instead of losing them."

Don't show the full dashboard, the inbox, AI drafts, billing, or anything else. One pain, one fix, two minutes. Everything else is a distraction on visit one.

### The ask
"Can I set this up for you free for 14 days? No card needed. I'll come back in a week and show you what it caught." 

Get their phone number and best day/time to return. If they hesitate: "You don't have to change anything you're doing — this just runs alongside it for two weeks."

### Objection handling

**"We already use WhatsApp for free."**
"You do, and that's exactly why this works well for you — it's the same WhatsApp your patients already have. The difference is you're not the one remembering to send reminders or chasing no-shows by hand — it happens automatically, and every patient conversation stays in one place even if two people are answering messages."

**"We don't have time to learn new software."**
"Totally fair — that's why I do the setup myself. You don't need to learn anything to receive the benefit; your front desk just keeps working the way they already do, and this runs underneath it."

**"How much does it cost?"**
"Free for the first 14 days, then it depends on how many staff and how much volume — but for a clinic your size it's usually ₹499-1,499 a month. I'd rather you see it work first and decide after."

**"We tried something like this before and it didn't work."**
"What happened with that one?" (Actually listen — this is the most valuable sentence you'll hear all week. Write it down after you leave.)

## 2. The Day 12-13 Trial-to-Paid Call

**Goal:** convert, on a call — not an email, not a text. Per the plan, this must be a personal call.

### Before the call
Check what actually happened in the trial: how many appointments were booked, how many reminders sent, how many no-shows the system caught or followed up on. You need at least one real number from their own data — this call doesn't work off a generic pitch.

### Opening
"Hi Dr. [Name], it's Sushant — just checking in on the trial. Do you have 5 minutes?"

### Lead with their data, not your pitch
"In the last two weeks, the system sent [N] reminders and caught [N] no-shows before they became a lost slot. At ₹[X] per consultation, that's roughly ₹[X×N] you didn't lose."

If usage was low: don't fake enthusiasm. Ask directly: "I noticed it wasn't used much this week — what got in the way?" Then decide together whether to extend the trial a few days or address whatever blocked adoption (usually: front desk wasn't shown how, or WhatsApp wasn't connected properly). A forced conversion on an unused trial churns in month one and teaches you nothing.

### The close
"Would you like to continue on the paid plan? It's ₹[price]/month, same as what you've been using — nothing changes on your end."

If yes: get them onto the plan on the call, don't leave it as a follow-up task for either of you.

### If they hesitate
"What's making you unsure?" — then actually stop talking. The answer here is either price, a missing feature, or "I forgot it existed" (an activation problem, not a sales problem — note it and go back to checking in earlier next time).

**Price objection:** "What would feel fair for what you saw it do?" Don't discount below the Starter tier reflexively — a clinic that got real value rarely balks at ₹499-1,499/month; if they do, that's a signal the value wasn't proven, not that the price is wrong.

**"Let me think about it."**
"Sure — can I call you Thursday? What would help you decide by then?" Never leave a maybe open-ended.

### After every call, log it
Update the weekly tracker from the plan (§6): trials started → activated → converted → churned. The clinics that say no are as valuable as the ones that say yes — the pattern in their objections is your product roadmap and your pitch rewrite, not noise to ignore.
